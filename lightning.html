When it happens

July is the most dangerous month, then June. Most deaths come between June and August, in the afternoon, with casualties peaking around 2 PM.

On the fourteeners

14 of Colorado's 332 lightning casualties from 1980 to 1999 happened on a fourteener, about 4%. Two strikes account for half of them: five hikers injured on Grays Peak in 1990, and three climbers hit near the summit of Capitol Peak in 1994, one fatally.

14 on fourteeners332 total · 1980–1999

The Colorado Fourteeners Initiative estimated about 200,000 people climbed fourteeners in 2000. The NWS incident logs match the two group strikes, but the original source for the 332 total and the 200,000 estimate still needs to be found.

Fewest deaths. Nevada had the fewest in the long-term record, 18 casualties from 1959 to 1994. In the recent decade, about ten states had a single death each, including Wyoming, Montana, North Dakota and South Dakota.

Check before publishing

  • Averages. Use either the computed 2.2 deaths and 10.9 injuries a year or the NWS's rounded 2 and 12, and cite which. The Denver Gazette's 3.5 deaths a year doesn't match its own totals.
  • Rank. Put the date range next to Colorado's rank every time. It ranges from 3rd to 11th depending on the period.
  • Fourteener figures. Find the original source for the 332 total and the 200,000-climber estimate, or drop them.
  • 2025. The Colorado totals run through 2024. Add 2025 once the NWS posts it.
